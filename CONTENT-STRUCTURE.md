**ROLL WITH ME — SUBPAGE AND CONTENT STRUCTURE**

Planning edition, 31 October 2026. This is the proposed page tree for developing content in later sessions. It supersedes the earlier high-level content outline where they differ. It does not claim that every proposed subpage, contact route or referral service is already implemented or staffed.

Source implementation update: the navigation, outlined topic/field pages, reserved story spaces and contact-form structure have since been written. See `docs/IMPLEMENTED-STRUCTURE.md` for the exact scope and remaining integration limits. The page-content planning below still applies.

The primary navigation remains: **Know the Rules / Choose a Path / Roll With Me / The Real**. Each opens a mobile hub with topic selection, swipeable introductions and a route to a full page. Help is available across all four rather than being treated as an unrelated destination.

**1. KNOW THE RULES**

**Purpose:** Understand the law, think through consequences, read situations and make safer choices. Know your rights. Move with awareness. Treat other people with dignity. Roll safe.

The landing page has two main doors: **THE LAW — rights, consequences and common sense** and **THE TERRAIN — everyday situations, your personal code and common sense**. Practical awareness and ethics complement legal information; they are not presented as substitutes for it.

**THE LAW: proposed subpages**

| Subpage | What the page is for |
| --- | --- |
| Police encounters & stop and search | What powers apply, questions to ask, your rights, how to seek advice, and what to do about unfair treatment. Distinguish routine suspicion-based powers from specifically authorised powers. |
| Arrest, interviews & a solicitor | Understand where to find legal advice, age-related safeguards and the importance of case-specific representation. |
| Violence, fights & consequences | Explain escalation, harm and responsibility without graphic material or attack instruction. Legal outcomes depend on facts, intent and evidence. |
| Knives & weapons | Explain legal risks and consequences without presenting blade rules as a protection loophole. Connect to leaving conflict and getting help. |
| Drugs, pressure & exploitation | Distinguish legal information, coercion and support. No instructions for selling, concealing or evading detection. |
| Consent, sex & relationships | Respect, freely made choices, age-related protections, positions of trust, pressure and support. Content must be age-appropriate and legally reviewed. |
| Online threats, harassment & private images | Show how digital conflict can escalate, explain relevant rights and provide safe support routes without asking for uploads of private images. |
| Friends, groups & shared responsibility | Explore peer pressure and legal responsibility through reviewed examples. Explain that a person's presence or friendships do not by themselves decide liability. |
| School, work & everyday rights | A place for reviewed school/college, apprenticeship, employment and money basics as the section grows. |

**THE TERRAIN: proposed subpages**

| Subpage | What the page is for |
| --- | --- |
| Read the situation | Notice a situation changing, consider space and exits, and seek safe public help. No territorial or gang-boundary maps. |
| How you carry yourself | Explore personal space, tone, gestures and situational awareness without prescribing a single correct appearance or eye-contact style. Account for disability, neurodivergence and cultural differences. |
| How you talk when things get tense | Short, optional de-escalation phrases and ways to leave without arguing or humiliating anyone. No promise that the right words can prevent another person's violence. |
| Moving through unfamiliar places | Plan public journeys, identify staffed places and know who you can contact. Do not ask users to share live location. |
| Out with friends / getting home later | Travel, checking plans, avoiding escalating group pressure and contacting a trusted person. Do not invent a general curfew or imply that being outside makes wrongdoing likely. |
| Your code: respect, loyalty & boundaries | Personal values, accountability, consent, looking after friends, refusing pressure and understanding that loyalty need not mean retaliation. No gang rules or criminal honour codes. |
| When online conflict moves offline | Avoid arranging confrontations, step back from escalating messages and get trusted support. |
| When to leave and when to get help | Prioritise leaving when safe, emergency alternatives and professional support. A harmed person is not responsible for another person's decision to harm them. |

**A shared Facts, Context & Myths resource** supports both branches. It holds reviewed explanations of stop/search disparities, victimisation, police-recorded offences, allegations and proven offending. These are different measures and must not be merged. Each statistic needs its source, geography, period, definition, denominator where relevant, missing-data limits and an explanation of what it cannot establish. No demographic figures are supplied by this outline.

Avoid offender-look images, unsupported racial profiles and advice that a young person must change their identity to deserve safety. Clothing or personal characteristics may appear in a specific reliable description, but stereotyping is not a substitute for legal grounds. The source material for this distinction includes PACE Code A, particularly paragraphs 2.2–2.6, and College of Policing guidance. Every published legal page still needs qualified review.

Official reference checked for the outline: [College of Policing — Fair](https://www.college.police.uk/app/stop-and-search/fair), [PACE Code A 2023](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/pace-code-a-december-2023/pace-code-a-2023-accessible), and [GOV.UK — Police powers to stop and search](https://www.gov.uk/police-powers-to-stop-and-search-your-rights/police-powers-stop-search).

**Common page pattern:** What this situation is; what the law says where relevant; what may happen in practice; choices that may reduce escalation; a short scenario; a private reflection; trusted sources/review record; and a context-specific support button. Keep rights, observation, ethical judgement and practical advice clearly labelled.

Examples for later reviewed scenario work include: a group waiting for transport; being approached in an unfamiliar area; an argument starting on a group chat; and a friend pressuring someone to stay. A phrase such as “I don't want any trouble. I'm heading home” can be one option, not a universal script or guaranteed protection. There is no duty to perform a particular eye-contact, speech or body-language style to deserve fair treatment.

**2. CHOOSE A PATH**

**Purpose:** Turn an interest into a realistic first step, then show possible routes into supervised practice, recognised learning and future work. The experience should answer: What is this? Could I enjoy it? How can I begin? Who can help?

The landing page has three main doors: **TECH & ENGINEERING / SPORTS / VOCATIONAL & PRACTICAL**.

| Main door | Proposed field pages |
| --- | --- |
| Tech & Engineering | Web development; software/coding; robotics & automation; electrical engineering; electronics; mechanical engineering; creative digital skills. |
| Sports | Football/futsal; boxing and martial arts through appropriate supervised programmes; athletics; basketball; coaching/refereeing and other sport careers. |
| Vocational & Practical | Motor vehicle mechanics; carpentry/joinery; electrical installation; plumbing/heating; construction/building trades; apprenticeships and entry routes. |

Keep electrical engineering, electronics and electrical installation distinct, with links between them. Likewise, distinguish mechanical engineering from motor vehicle repair while explaining their connections.

**Every field page follows the same pattern:**

1. What the field is, in plain English.
2. What people actually do, including several roles and routes rather than one promised career.
3. The first concepts worth learning.
4. An age-aware starting route for 13–15, 16–17 and 18–20. Do not make a blanket claim that every activity or job opens at 16.
5. A manageable first-week plan and a small, safe starter project.
6. A reviewed shortlist of beginner videos/resources. YouTube recommendations will be selected later, checked for quality, accessibility, age suitability and safety, and dated.
7. How to find a trusted introduction: family contact, school/college careers team, qualified provider or verified supervised programme.
8. Training routes and entry requirements: tasters, college, apprenticeships, university or work as applicable. Check each real opportunity rather than implying that all routes have the same requirements.
9. Relevant local opportunities, with area selection retained.
10. “Help me start this path” and “I'm a parent/carer asking about this path.”

A trusted relative or family friend may offer an introduction to a field. Practical work, tools, vehicle workshops, machinery and electrical projects still require suitable supervision, permissions, safety arrangements and role/age eligibility. Do not direct a child into unsupervised work or hazardous tasks as a generic next step.

This stage defines page slots; it does not prescribe specific video links, course providers, pay, prices or guaranteed places.

**3. ROLL WITH ME — LOCAL ACTIVITIES**

**Purpose:** Give someone a real, checked next thing to do and make the route from interest to participation understandable.

**Page sequence:** Choose North / East / West / South London; choose borough; choose activity category or browse all; swipe the available activities; open a verified listing; then follow its approved registration route.

Area and borough choice should remain easy to change throughout this section. They are manual preferences, not live location. London directions are browsing shortcuts and may overlap.

| Subpage or view | What it contains |
| --- | --- |
| Area landing | A choice of North, East, West and South London, with a brief explanation of manual area selection. |
| Borough view | Available verified activities plus category filters; a clear empty state when nothing is published. |
| Sport & movement | Football, basketball, boxing fitness, athletics and other approved activity types. |
| Tech & engineering activities | Coding, robotics, electronics and supervised maker programmes. |
| Creative activities | Film, editing, photography, animation, music and design. |
| Trades, careers & enterprise | Practical tasters, careers support, verified apprenticeships where relevant and supervised enterprise programmes. |
| Mentoring & support opportunities | Vetted, appropriately supervised programmes, not an unrestricted directory for private adult-to-child contact. |
| Holidays, events & challenges | Age-specific seasonal sessions and supervised challenges. |
| Individual activity page | Provider, public venue, date/time, age eligibility, cost, accessibility, capacity/booking state, consent, transport, verification/last check, and contact/official route. |
| Registration & booking | Age/eligibility check, necessary account/guardian steps, transparent emergency-contact use, confirmation or waiting-list status and help joining. |

Free, holiday, date, accessibility, interest and age are filters rather than separate versions of every page. A learning article can link into a relevant local category without making a place or provider appear verified automatically.

**4. THE REAL — STORIES**

**Purpose:** Make space for honest experiences and turning points, connect choices to consequences and show where support or another opportunity can fit.

The launch planning board has **ten story slots, ST01–ST10, awaiting the user's material**. Do not invent ten real-person accounts or treat existing fictional demo stories as supplied testimony.

| Subpage | What it contains |
| --- | --- |
| Story hub | Swipeable story introductions and theme selection. |
| Theme/series view | Related stories grouped after the ten contributions have been reviewed. Possible themes include pressure, decisions, aftermath, support and a new direction; final themes should follow the actual material. |
| Individual story | Appropriate content note; approved text, animation or video; captions/transcript; experience and context; consequences; learning point; support route; and related activity or pathway. |
| Private reflection | An optional “What would you do?” or “What could help?” prompt. Responses are not public comments. |
| Share a story | An initial expression of interest only, with human screening, permissions and review before any publication. Do not invite public traumatic disclosures or evidence uploads. |

For each supplied story, record whether it is a first-person account, anonymised account, scripted composite or animation; permissions and any guardian consent; source confidence; editorial/safeguarding review; age suitability; and publication restrictions. No identifiable minor victims, graphic violence, current gang identifiers or perpetrator mythology.

**5. HELP AND CONTACT ACROSS EVERY SECTION**

The shared contact destination can retain the current name **Ask RWM**, with a clearer button label such as **Get help with this**. Context links should carry only the relevant topic/page, not a hidden activity history.

Every substantive page provides three routes: read a relevant trusted support option; send a general question/request to RWM; or contact RWM as a parent/carer on someone's behalf. A separate visible urgent-help route does not depend on waiting for RWM.

**Proposed contact entry pages:**

| Entry | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| I want to ask for myself | A young person can ask about rights, a pathway, an activity or support without creating a public profile. |
| I'm a parent/carer asking on someone's behalf | Collect the adult's relationship, general concern and safe reply details. This does not grant access to a youth account, private messages or survey answers. |
| Find a trusted service | Curated, checked service cards with purpose, age range, coverage, official contact channels, availability and last-checked date. |
| I need urgent help | Clear emergency and trusted-service routes. Immediate UK danger: 999. This is separate from ordinary intake. |

**Minimal request fields:** who is contacting; broad topic; relevant age band where needed; borough only when useful for referral; short general description; a safe reply email if a response is wanted; safe-contact preferences; and an explanation/acknowledgement of information use. Ask for a phone number only if an appropriately staffed callback service exists. Avoid names/locations of other children, detailed allegations, offence admissions, traumatic narratives or uploads in the initial form.

**Message and email routing:** A web form and an official RWM support mailbox should feed the same restricted human triage process. The mailbox address must be created and approved after the domain and operator are confirmed. None is invented by this plan. Explain that ordinary email is unsuitable for highly sensitive submissions. Do not publish personal volunteer email addresses for direct private contact with children.

**The follow-up process:** Request received; neutral acknowledgement and request reference; human triage; assignment to the appropriate approved team; agreement on a safe follow-up route; reply or referral; and a recorded next step. Separate routes are needed for legal advice, careers/pathways, activities/booking and safeguarding/wellbeing. Gather consent before routine sharing with an outside partner; explain lawful safeguarding exceptions. A parent submission does not automatically authorise disclosure of the young person's private information back to that parent.

Response hours and time targets must match real staffing. Do not promise 24/7 monitoring, absolute confidentiality or a guaranteed callback before a service exists. If contact details are omitted, explain that a direct reply is not possible. A confirmation message must distinguish receipt from completed referral or resolution. Urgent or high-risk cases require trained human assessment and external escalation as appropriate; no AI auto-answering or automatic resolution.

**6. HOW TO DEVELOP THE CONTENT NEXT**

First, develop Know the Rules page by page: agree the objective, audience, scenario, legal facts, practical advice, sources, reviewer and support route for each. Second, flesh out each Choose a Path field, selecting real beginner resources and verified next steps. Third, add the ten supplied stories through consent and review. Populate local activities as real providers are checked. Map every page to a relevant support category.

Keep one content planning record per page: ID; section/parent page; working title; purpose; age suitability; planned format; key questions; source/video requirements; owner; required review; related pages/activities; support route; and status. Statuses should distinguish an empty slot, outlined page, drafted content, pending review and approved content.

This outline changes the planned subpage structure. The previous source inventory still describes what is presently in the repository. New pages, contact categories, parent-on-behalf fields, mailbox integration and follow-up workflows require implementation and testing rather than being assumed to exist already.
